I am a Senior Machine Learning Scientist at [Matterworks](https://www.matterworks.ai), where I build foundation models for mass spectrometry. My work sits at the intersection of AI × Science: building, scaling, and deploying models that learn from messy, high-dimensional scientific data. At Matterworks, I developed the core architecture and self-supervised pretraining for the Large Spectral Model (LSM), and led the development of our small molecule identification models.

Drawn to hard problems in any corner of STEM, I began my career with a B.S.E. in chemical engineering and a minor in computer science from the University of Michigan. During undergrad I explored a wide breadth of problems, from leading teams building aerodynamic wind turbine blades to interning at a large industrial gas supplier, before landing at ExxonMobil to model refinery kinetics. Quickly seeing the potential of machine learning in the field, I was pulled back to school for an M.S. in computer science at the University of Wisconsin. My graduate research in the Van Lehn Laboratory focused on geometric deep learning methods for molecular dynamics simulations. Set on pursuing frontier research in AI × Science, I joined Ford after graduation as an ML researcher working on an automated materials discovery lab. That work was later published at a NeurIPS workshop, where it was selected as a spotlight talk. I then joined Matterworks as the third ML researcher, hoping to build a research-driven product from the ground up. Over the past three years I have explored a field of mass spectrometry largely untapped by machine learning, leading projects from brainstorming through customer deployment.

My key research interests include:

- *Foundation models for scientific data:* Multimodal pretraining over spectra, molecules, and structure, and what it takes for those representations to transfer across tasks.
- *World models:* Learned simulators of physical and chemical systems, accurate enough to design against rather than only predict with.
- *Grounding models in physics:* Building physical law into architectures and training objectives, and understanding when it helps and when it does not.
- *Automated discovery loops:* Closing the loop between generative models, simulation, and experiment, so that a system proposes and tests its own candidates.
- *Machine learning for engineering and hardware:* Applying modern methods to the process, mechanical, and hardware problems where classical modeling still dominates.

Above all, what drives me is tackling tough problems that have a positive impact on society, and challenging myself to learn something new in the process. My diverse STEM background makes it so that the domain alone matters less to me than the problem — chemistry, biology, physics, or something I have not worked in yet, each drawing my interest. Always happy to connect and chat!
